# Three hard datums, one conditional compliant frame — NOFAB

This is a bounded alternative to the previous up-to-22-new-piece individually fitted shell study. It does not approve a 19 or 20 mm final pen, replace the requested 15–18 mm goal, or qualify manufacturing, battery/camera integration, switch life, materials, cost or safety. Four shell bodies still do not form a complete product: the front component is only an open interface collar, no optical nose/cap latch is defined, and the original RF all-Y exclusion prism conflicts with a continuous dielectric shell.

## Physical count and datum logic

Proposed new assembly: four main bodies, one cassette keeper, one single-piece peripheral compliant frame, three M1.6×8 reference fasteners = nine new pieces. This excludes pre-existing cassette/board/pen internals, wiring, unfinished bare-tail guards and any temporary assembly tooling. No individually thickness-fitted shims are selected. Cost is unknown: custom material/process, threads, labour, optical nose, battery fastening, insulation protection and supply quotes are not zero-cost.

PCB B reference Y=2.2647931763 mm. Three rigid support strips are left/right Z45 and left Z131, each 0.25 mm inward from the PCB edge, Z±1. Right rear Z131 is a catch support 0.1 mm below B, not a fourth hard clamping datum. Three Y fingers preload directly above the three supports. Two right-side fingers push toward the left X locators. One rear Z finger pushes toward the head stop. The single part therefore contains **six distinct compliant features**, not one scalar spring. Frame roots/long rails are not proven rigid.

The F-side toe cylinder spans X[-7.35,-6.75] or [6.75,7.35], so only 0.25 mm overlaps the actual X±7 PCB; Z projection ±0.4 lies inside the previously reviewed Z±1 strips. Beam width 0.6 mm is not incorrectly substituted for contact width. Side/tail touch projections are as frozen in `compliant_frame_edge_contact_screen_0441`, manifest 8ba6bb3e5b649e3b9ee6a048ff6b6daa16b9b093a36e13616467a2c120fb48a6. Filled-copper separations .3 /4.38646 /.2505 mm are geometry, NOT approved pressure, abrasion, edge strain, insulation or creep performance. The original F/B edge-strip inner-layer separation .05 mm remains a damage gate.

## Thickness and force bounds

Only nominal .83 mm and maximum .93 mm PCB thickness are source-supported. No minimum .73 mm is invented. The free Y toe is B+.75, giving +Y geometric deflections .08/.18 mm for the two samples and a prospective −Y restoring preload. A board ≤.75 mm gives no positive preload; actual local thickness, frame height, warp and force must be measured and accepted or rejected. No bending of the PCB to fit four hard points is allowed.

Y hard-stop geometric travel is .30 mm, leaving .12 mm beyond the .93 thickness pose before considering print tolerances/installation errors. This is NOT proof that the full manufactured tolerance distribution is accommodated. X nominal/limit travel .05/.15 mm; Z .10/.40 mm; these are separate kinematic models with unknown material limits.

Conditional slender-beam model, rigid root, rectangular section, incremental small-deflection cantilever: I=b*t^3/12, F/E=b*t^3*delta/(4*L^3), maximum surface strain estimate=1.5*t*delta/L^2. Y b=.6,t=.7,L=8; X b=.4,t=.25,L=6; Z b=.4,t=.25,L=8 (all mm). No E, allowable stress/strain, preload range, fatigue life or creep limit is selected. The generated cubic poses are not an FEA solution and their small volume differences do not imply physical material compressibility. Actual global-frame bending/torsion, precurvature and printed anisotropy invalidate treating those local coefficients as measured forces.

Required matched-process coupons and assembled measurements: force/displacement through free→nominal→maximum→stop; root/rail twist; preload retention after time/temperature/humidity; PCB local curvature and electrical continuity; edge/coating damage; deliberate removal and repeated reassembly; wire/joint motion and fatigue. Display resolution is not measurement uncertainty. No arbitrary .01 mm PCB displacement acceptance or 5 µm instrument purchase is imposed. Optical-head/refill mechanical accuracy must be established separately from PCB retention.

## Assembly / service sequence to validate

1. Verify and calibrate the real cassette and hard stops using actual ON/OFF/hysteresis records; reject always-closed or inadequate-overtravel specimens. Then assemble the cassette with its left keeper and existing horizontal fasteners before lowering both together into the lower datum body. Do not force the keeper alone across preinstalled cross-heads.
2. Seat the board onto the three B datums and left/head hard stops without wiring forces. Measure local thickness and warp. Introduce the frame in a deliberately pre-deflected configuration so Y toes, side toes and rear toe do not scrape/overstrain the PCB. A real hold-open fixture/tool and safe force are not yet selected; simulated installed-pose insertion is not proof this manual operation is safe.
3. Tighten the two Y-axis reference fasteners while checking three-point contact, PCB curvature and frame alignment. The nonlocating upper cover is not used to maintain either short-wire endpoint. Verify the cassette axial fastener independently. No load path through battery, components, solder joints or wires.
4. **BLOCKED soldering step:** the retained service openings only address the carrier near Z78.4. The proposed mainboard B70.5 lands are at Z56.5; with the board already on the three B datums, a +Y tool is blocked by the PCB and no underside/side shell service path has been established. Therefore this study does NOT yet give an executable post-retention two-end soldering sequence. The three driver-budget tests are fastening access, not soldering access. Actual tip/holding/stripping/cleaning and bare-tail protection must be solved before this step can be performed. A separate common fixture for pre-soldering and subsequent transfer is only an untested alternative; it cannot be assumed to preserve calibration or avoid loading the wires.
5. Remove upper cover without releasing datum fasteners. If cassette replacement requires releasing calibrated geometry, first desolder/secure the wires and repeat calibration and endpoint installation; do not pull the wire joints to extract the part.

The M1.6 reference solids use simplified major-diameter clearance geometry, not generated usable threads. A potential physical process is cutting-tap M1.6×.35 with Ø1.25 pilot (prior primary Gühring source), NOT forming-tap substitution. Exact engagement, outside wall, tool access and pilot machining must be dimensioned from the final accepted solids; strength, tightening torque, pullout and creep require process/material specimens. No metal insert is silently added near RF.

### Measurable force gate (not yet supplied)

Measure actual assembled PCB mass m, the normal acceleration/handling envelope a_normal, maximum wire reaction F_wire and frame relaxation under environment/time. A necessary normal retention check is total effective preload > m(g+a_normal)+F_wire, with a separately justified safety margin and load distribution; it is not sufficient for local bending or X/Z restraint. Also measure each support/edge allowable contact pressure and coating damage, anchor force capacity, and retention after creep. No mass, acceleration, E, friction coefficient or safety factor is invented here. Preload must be large enough for retention yet below measured board/edge/insulator damage limits. Geometric .08/.18 mm interference alone does not establish that a feasible force window exists.

## Explicit wall and root-seat evidence

`wall_sections.json` contains exact native ray/solid intersections, not a claimed global minimum. At rail/channel corner (X7.9,Y4.5), residual outer-connected wall is .408245 mm for OD19 and .908245 mm for OD20. OD19 retains intentional open service notches at Z45/131 (zero wall at the toe/limit rays), not an allegedly safe paper wall. At the OD20 side-limit corner (8.05,4.5,Z45), remaining wall is .777609 mm; Y-limit corner (7.9,4.665) leaves .825458 mm. At the cassette plate-pocket corner (−7.65,−4.05,Z64), OD19 leaves .844077 mm and OD20 leaves its original 1.3 mm shell wall. Both retain the open solder service ports (zero wall), with sweat/dirt/touch ingress unqualified. These are evidence of space tradeoffs, not material-strength minima or final enclosure approval. Exact native section PNG/SVG were generated and visually inspected.

Two integral lower-body root seats have plane Y3.35, X/Z patches [-6.75,-4.25]×[29.25,31.75] and [-5.05,-2.55]×[134.95,137.45], excluding shaft radius .85. They physically touch the frame underside, rather than leaving .10 mm clearance for screws to pull the frame down. The two current Y screw axes are (X−5.5,Z30.5) and (X−3.8,Z136.2). No individually selected shim does NOT mean zero fitting labour: seat-height errors, frame warp and free-toe manufacturing variation all change preload. Measure both seats, roots and three contacts; reject or revise the single retainer if outside the validated force window. Do not assume every printed part produces simultaneous acceptable preload.

Rounded Y toes use R.4 cylinders; side/tail toes use R.3. Their quoted rectangular projections bound copper proximity, **not actual load-bearing contact area**. Ideal rigid-cylinder/plane contact is a line; real contact width/pressure depends on force and the two materials. Do not divide force by the projection rectangle and claim an acceptable pressure. Local indentation, solder-mask damage and edge laminate damage require the actual material/force specimen test; this may require a different toe form later. Likewise geometric stop contact is not an infinitely rigid force-rated overload stop: the shared frame and stop walls can deflect.
